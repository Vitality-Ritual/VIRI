# VIRI — terms of service: known weak points

**Internal. Not published, and not to be published.**

Margaret decided on 3 October 2026 to publish the terms without legal review,
knowing and accepting the risk. This file is the record of what is being
accepted, kept for two reasons: so the decision was made with the list in
view rather than in ignorance of it, and so that if a lawyer is ever engaged
they can start here instead of reading everything from scratch.

It stays out of the published terms deliberately. An inventory of the places
a liability waiver might fail is not something to hand to the people it is
meant to work against.

## The open questions

1. **This is the document carrying the whole risk.** No entity has been formed,
   so liability is personal and unlimited. Section 10 and the assumption of risk
   in section 5 are the only protection that exists. If one clause here gets
   professional attention, those.

2. **Waiver enforceability.** Section 5 asks members to accept the risk of
   meeting strangers. Enforceability varies by state and can fail on wording,
   prominence or assent. Needs review against DC law and the states members are
   likely to be in. Gross negligence and wilful misconduct generally cannot be
   waived — section 10 should probably say so explicitly rather than rely on the
   catch-all.

3. **Assent.** Sign-up uses a tick box that is required and names both
   documents, with links. Confirm this clears clickwrap standards, and whether
   the terms need to be scrollable at the point of agreement rather than linked.

4. **No identity verification — disclosed, but is disclosure enough?** Section 4
   states plainly that nobody is checked. Worth advice on whether a product that
   introduces women to strangers for in-person meetings attracts a duty of care
   that disclosure alone does not discharge, and whether any screening is
   expected as a standard of care.

5. **Who may join — decided: women only.** Section 2a now states it, sign-up
   requires an affirmative confirmation, and trans women are explicitly
   included. Self-declared; nothing is verified, and the terms say so.
   **This now needs clearance rather than a decision:** a stated membership
   restriction engages the DC Human Rights Act's public accommodation
   provisions in a way a mere statement of purpose does not. Please advise
   whether it is permissible as drafted, and if not, what would be. Much
   easier to adjust before launch than after.

6. **Arbitration and class waiver — deliberately omitted.** Standard in consumer
   terms and would reduce exposure. Left out because forcing a woman harmed
   after meeting through VIRI into private arbitration is an ethical and
   reputational decision, not merely a legal one, and it should be made
   knowingly rather than inherited from a template. Advise on the trade-off.

7. **The $100 cap** is conventional for a free service but arbitrary. With no
   entity behind it, consider whether it is worth anything or whether insurance
   is the real answer.

8. **The two-business-day commitment** in section 5 is published deliberately,
   as a trust signal for a safety product, but it is a promise that can be
   missed. Worded as an aim rather than a guarantee. Confirm that wording
   is sufficient, and whether publishing any response time is wise at all.

9. **Insurance.** No cover is in place. General liability and tech E&O should be
   priced before launch; a waiver and an insurance policy do different jobs.

10. **Minors.** 18+ is enforced by a required birth-year field whose options
   start at 18. Self-declared, no verification. Confirm that is sufficient.

11. **Termination without notice** (section 8) is drafted broadly to allow fast
    action on safety reports. Confirm it is not so broad as to be unfair-terms
    exposure.

12. **Studio listings.** Named businesses and their photographs appear without
    partnership. Section 4 disclaims affiliation; confirm that is adequate, and
    see the outstanding image-rights question separately.
